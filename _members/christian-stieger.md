Christian studied chemistry at the University of Vienna and completed his master's thesis on structural proteomics in the group of Karl Mechtler at the Research Institute of Molecular Pathology (IMP).
He then moved to Berlin for his doctoral research in the group of Prof. Christian Hackenberger at the Leibniz-Forschungsinstitut für Molekulare Pharmakologie (FMP), where he advanced the development and application of unsaturated phosphorus(V)-based electrophiles for protein bioconjugation, covalent drug development and activity-based protein profiling.

Supported by a Walter Benjamin Fellowship from the German Research Foundation (DFG), he joined Daniel Nomura's group at the University of California, Berkeley.
His research there focused on several induced-proximity platforms, including the development of small-molecule-based bifunctional compounds to reprogram gene expression in cancer cells.

Since January 2027 he is a Junior Research Group Leader at TUM, supported by a Liebig Fellowship from the Fonds der Chemischen Industrie.
